Curve: Strong, well-sequenced teaching spine: merge, orders, crates, Big Merge, fireworks, thread, needle, recipes, keys, jars, sweep, staged and chain orders. Each is introduced alone and then combined, with a boss at L13, L26, L39, L52 and L64. The weaknesses are these.

1) Every level is a tiny single-insight "count the pieces" puzzle with a fixed solution. The stated budgets are often exact (Sparks 2/2, 3/3), so the set reads as worksheet-like against Royal Match or Flow Free variety. Almost every level is also a "don't merge by habit" trap.

2) Big Merge is overused as the aha. It appears in L8, 9, 10, 12, 13, 21, 25, 37, 39, 46, 56, 59 and 60, so it goes stale.

3) Breathers are mis-placed or too thin. L20 is good. L34 is a very hard-to-parse board right after a trunk run. L42 is a "breather" with a jar-plus-trunk mechanic. L48 and L55 are fine but are not clearly easier than their neighbours, and L55 casual 89% is only slightly above.

4) Early flatness. L1-L7 are all 99-80% and L8 is 100%, so levels 5-8 have little tension. The easy-run rule is respected only because L6, L7 and L5 dip below 90%.

5) Late curve: the casual average over the last third (L43-L64) is roughly 60%, on target. Spikes: L45, L49, L51, L52, L54 and L64 (43% casual, 77% skilled) are hard stacked-combination levels. L64 as a finale with casual 43% is acceptable but punishing. L52 has a 'search cap' solution, so its fairness is unverified.

6) Late hand-holding. Many descriptions say 'the start hint says it outright', which means the fix is delivered by text rather than by board readability.

7) Gaps in the pacing of the plan. L62-L64 finales are three dense levels back to back. L62 is clearly a "full board" test but its aha is an obscure mechanic (nearest empty cell), and there is no breather before them.
