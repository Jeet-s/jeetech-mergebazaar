FAIL level_design: level-design review 7.4/10 (bar 8), 0 level(s) below 6/10: This is a well-engineered set. Every level has a stated aha, the intents mostly match the boards, and each board has a replayed proof. The late-game difficulty is on target: the last-third casual average is about 64% and the skilled rates are 77-97%. The weaknesses are these:
- **Counting theme overused early.** L2-L6 all teach "count what's missing" with a decoy.
- **Decoy-row Sweep repeated.** The "pick the row with the most blockers" puzzle appears in L43, L50, L52 and L56.
- **Chain orders out of order.** The pays/chain-order mechanic is used in L51 before L57 teaches it, then L57-L61 are five chain-order levels in a row.
- **Hidden fatal first moves.** L36, L44, L50, L56, L19 and L15 have trap rates of 0.14-0.33. Several of these fatal first moves are only recoverable through Undo.
- **Boards are tiny puzzle boxes.** Most boards are 3x3 to 5x5 with 5-14 moves, so they read as puzzles, not the 7x9 generator-merge sessions the design doc promises. The core loop of clutter, energy pacing, Bag and Sort is never tested.
- **Breather placement.** L21 and L34 follow levels that are already easy, so they don't relieve any difficulty. The real difficulty spike (L19 at 60%) has no rest after it.
- **Boosters are thin.** Each booster is taught once and used a few times, and the Bag, Sort and Undo are never needed.

The finale trio (L62-L64) is strong and memorable. Curve: The intro run (L1-L13) is clean and well paced. It runs merge, generator, order tray, crates, Big Merge, Fireworks and then a boss, with each mechanic introduced alone first. Casual win rates fall smoothly from 99% to 78%. The weak spot is L2-L6, which are five levels that all teach "count what's missing". L5 and L6 in particular feel like L2/L3 again with different art.

Thread (L14-L17, then L20-L23) and recipes (L18-L19) are introduced one at a time and combined sensibly. Recipes arrive late, at L18, even though the core loop leans on them, so consider moving them earlier. L19 is an isolated spike at 60% casual between 85% and 93%, and it is driven by a trap, not by depth. L21 (the first breather) comes after a 93% level, not after the hard one.

Jars (L24, L40, L44, L45), trunks (L27-L30, L33, L36, L53-L55) and 3-input recipes (L31-L32) are each taught cleanly. L33-L36 is a good arc (story, breather, teach, combine). The weak spots are L36 and L44, which carry a 0.33 trap rate each.

The back half is where pacing slips:
- **Staged orders.** Staged orders are taught at L41, then used in L45-L46.
- **Chain orders.** Chain orders (pays) are first needed in L51, which is a COMBINE level. They are only taught at L57, which comes after the run that needed them, then five chain-order levels (L57-L61) arrive in a row, so the mechanic is stale before the finale.
- **Decoy-row Sweep puzzles.** These repeat across L43, L50, L52 and L56.
- **Difficulty targets.** L43 is a teach level at 73% casual, which is too low for a teach. The last-third average of about 64% and skilled rates of 77-97% meet the studio targets, but several levels hit them through traps and hidden-info gaps (casual versus skilled gaps of 35-40 points in L45, L49 and L62), not through depth.
- **Easy streaks.** No level breaks the easy-streak rule, since two consecutive levels at 90% or above is the maximum.
- **Finale.** The finale trio (L62-L64) is a strong crescendo: deliver to free the cell, then every merge does two jobs, then the chain spine. L64 at 43% casual is brutal but acceptable for a grand finale.

Fix list: re-order the chain-order teach before L51, spread the chain levels with non-chain ones, soften the traps with a spare Spark, and add a real breather after L19.
