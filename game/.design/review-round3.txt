FAIL level_design: level-design review 7.4/10 (bar 8), 2 level(s) below 6/10: This is a strong, well-explained set. Nearly every level has a stated aha, and the solver facts match the intents. The best levels (L24, L29, L35, L53, L54, L62, L64) reach the quality of the genre leaders, with clear "I see it now" moments.

Main weaknesses:
- Two tricks are repeated too often: Big Merge (the "gather five" idea) and the decoy generator or crate.
- Several teach and story levels carry high hidden-trap rates: L20 (0.5), L25 (0.5), L21 (0.4), L31 (0.33), L47 (0.33), L16 (0.2). A player's first natural move can silently doom the level.
- L25's intent does not match the board.
- Boss difficulty is inconsistent: L13 and L39 are too soft, while L59 is an outlier spike.
- The last-third casual average is about 65.4%, just over the 65% cap.
- Some breathers and teach levels are not easier than their neighbours (L48, L58).

Fix those and the set is a solid 8. Curve: Pacing is mostly sound. Mechanics arrive one at a time and then combine: merge and generator (1-3), crates (4-7), Big Merge (8-10), Fireworks (11-13), thread (14-17), recipes (18-22), Needle and breather (16, 23), Big Merge plus recipe (24-26), trunks and keys (27-30), 3-input recipes (31-34), trunk plus Big Merge plus key (35-39), jars (40-43), staged orders (44-46), Sweep (47-51), wrapped trunks (53-56), mixed and chain orders (57-61), finale (62-64). Boss levels land every 13 levels (13, 26, 39, 52), and the finale uses everything. Breathers are well placed at 23, 34, 42, 48 and 60.

Problems:
1. Big Merge is overused. It is the aha of about 15 levels (8, 9, 10, 12, 13, 20, 24, 25, 37, 39, 41, 46, 55, plus 34/60 in lighter form). "Gather five, merge once, the bonus is the next input" repeats from L9 onward. L8 to L10 are three in a row, which is too many. L12 and L55 share the same "crate makes the fifth piece" trap.
2. "Decoy generator, so don't tap it" is the aha in L2, 3, 7, 15, 29, 31, 55, 62 and 63. This is the cheapest trick in the set. Because the Spark budgets are tight, several of these decoys are a fatal tap rather than a lost star.
3. Boss difficulty is uneven. L13 (87% casual) and L39 (81%) are soft for bosses. L26 (57%) and L52 (49%) land correctly. L59 (42%) is a "practise" level that is the hardest in its neighbourhood, between L58 (69%) and L60 (81%).
4. The last-third casual average is about 65.4%. That is just over the 65% cap, so lift a few late levels slightly. The levels to ease are L59 and L64 (a little), or raise L47/L48/L60.
5. Hidden-fatal-move rates are too high in several teach and early levels: L20 (0.5), L25 (0.5), L21 (0.4), L31 (0.33), L16 (0.2) and L47 (0.33). A teach level should never punish the player's first natural guess.
6. L25's intent does not match its board, which has no middle spool. Its Big Merge plan also cannot fill the Cotton Cloth order.
7. The Dye Stand costs 2 Sparks and is introduced at L19. It is then used as a price decoy in L21, L26 and L59, which is fine, but tutorialise Spark prices earlier.
8. There is no real difficulty rise from L1 to L12, where every level wins at about 90% or more. L17 (67%) and L19 (62%) are early spikes next to 82-99% neighbours.
9. Late levels run long. L58 (14 moves) is a teach, and L38, L61, L63 and L64 (14-17 moves) are long for a 3-10 minute session. This is acceptable for finales but heavy for L58.

Overall the set is well-crafted and cohesive, with strong late-game aha levels (L24, L29, L35, L53, L54, L62). It needs de-duplication of the Big Merge and decoy tricks, plus a fairness pass on the hidden-fatal levels.
