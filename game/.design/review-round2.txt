FAIL level_design: level-design review 7/10 (bar 8), 5 level(s) below 6/10: This is a strong, unusually well-documented set. Almost every level has a stated insight, and the best ones are memorable: L15 (junk pair vs needed pair), L39 (five keys Big-Merge into filigree plus silver), L42 (the jar holds a key), L62 (the cell you empty is where the next piece lands), and L61. The weaknesses fall into six groups.

1) Big Merge is the crutch. It appears in about 15 levels (8, 9, 10, 12, 13, 20, 24, 25, 34, 37, 39, 41, 46, 55, 60) and keeps the same corner-plus-centre-generator layout.

2) Several levels say one thing and the board says another. In these the intent text, the Spark budget and the proof disagree: L28 (a "third Spark" that doesn't exist), L29, L34 ("a Spark to spare" with 1/1 used), L48, L55 and L47. The wrong choice is often forgiven, so the aha is never enforced.

3) A few early hidden-fatal moves, in L2, L17 and L20, rely on the one-step Undo to feel fair.

4) Difficulty is lumpy. L21 and L24-L28 form a cluster of 55-60% casual wins around story and practise slots, and L59 is an isolated 46%/68% dip between 69% and 81% neighbours. L64 at 33% casual is a wall.

5) Mechanic introduction is mostly sound, but staged, chain and mixed-recipe orders arrive at L44, L57 and L58. That leaves little practise time before the three finales.

6) The doc promises a 7x9 board and unhurried play, but no level exceeds 7x7. Hard Spark budgets also read as pressure rather than coziness.

The last-third casual average is about 65%, right at the ceiling. It is acceptable, but it leaves no headroom. Curve: Chapter structure (about 13 levels each, bosses at 13, 26, 39, 52 and 64) is clear. The order is mostly sound: generators and merge (1-3), crates (4-7), Big Merge (8-10), Fireworks (11), thread and Needle (14-17), recipes (18-22), trunks and keys (27-30), 3-input recipes (31-33), jars (40-43), staged orders (44-46), Sweep (47-48), wrapped trunks (53-54), mixed recipes (57), chain orders (58-61), then the finales.

Problems:
(a) L1-L13 is a long, near-flat run of 86-100% casual wins, with a weak boss at L13 (87%). The only spike is L11 (69%), a Fireworks teach with 20 start choices.
(b) Big Merge is reused far too often and becomes the default aha. L12, L37 and L55 repeat "deliver or pair before the fifth piece arrives". L41, L43 and L52 repeat "one cell touches all the blockers". L20 and L26 share the same walled-corner geometry.
(c) The L21-L28 stretch is bumpy. It runs 57%, 76%, 100%, 60%, 58%, 57%, 93%, 55%, with the lows at story or practise slots and the highs at breathers. Story beats should not be 58%. L28 follows a 93% teach with a 55% practise that introduces key merging.
(d) Staged orders arrive late (L44), and mixed recipes (L57) and chain orders (L58) come very late. L57 and L58 are teaches played with zero Spark slack, at 71% and 69% casual, which is too harsh for a teach. The L62-L64 finales then test all of them at once.
(e) L59 is an isolated difficulty dip (46%/68%) between 69% and 81% neighbours.
(f) Breathers are inconsistent. L23, L34, L42 and L60 work. L29 and L48 don't (L48 is nearly identical to L47), and L35 (a story beat) is a free ride.
(g) The last third averages about 65% casual, which is on target but at the ceiling. L64 at 33% casual is a wall for a grand finale; the whole set would feel better with the finale around 45%.
(h) A fail state driven by a Spark budget sits awkwardly with the "unhurried, cozy" promise. Tight 5/5 and 3/3 budgets with no slack appear across L38, L46, L52, L57 and L58.
