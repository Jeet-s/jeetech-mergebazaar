FAIL level_design: level-design review 7/10 (bar 8), 1 level(s) below 6/10: This is a thoughtful, unusually well-documented set. Nearly every level has one clear idea and an intent the board actually backs up. The mechanic ladder is clean: merge, then crates, Big Merge, thread, recipes, trunks, jars, staged orders, chain orders. The bosses and finales combine earlier ideas well, and L12, L29, L34, L42, L62 and L64 are memorable. Five problems stop it from being top-tier.
1. The "gather five before you merge" aha is overused. L8, 9, 12, 13, 20, 24, 25, 37, 39, 41, 46 and 55 all lean on it, and L9, L24 and L46 reuse the same "bonus piece is the next input" insight.
2. The crate lesson is taught three times in a row (L4, L5, L10). L55 is L12 reskinned.
3. Several mechanics arrive too late. Wrapped trunks come at L53, recipe stacking at L57 and chain orders at L58. The finales depend on that last group, which has only 3-4 levels of ramp.
4. Late difficulty is slightly off target. The last-third (L43-64) casual average is about 65.3%, just over the ≤65% cap. L44, L47, L48 and L60 are soft (81-85%) and drag the average up.
5. The boards are tiny. Most are 3x3 to 5x4, with optimal solves of 5-14 moves (under a minute), against a doc promising a 7x9 board and 3-10 minute sessions. That is a Monument Valley-style puzzle feel, not Royal Match or Homescapes session depth.
Smaller issues: the Dye Stand costs 1 Spark in L18 and L57 but 2 Sparks elsewhere. Story-beat markers stop after L35, against a "story beat every ~5 levels" promise. L9 and L10 have hidden early fatal moves. Curve: The first third is excellent. Merge, then generator, then orders, then crates, then Big Merge arrive one at a time, each in a readable board. Chapter 1 ends in a boss at L13 that combines crates, a Firework and Big Merge. Chapter 2 (thread, Needle, recipes) climbs well, with L19-L22 as a decent spike and L23 as a real breather.
The curve then develops three problems.
1. Repetition of the aha. Big Merge is the answer to a quarter of the levels. Crates are taught three times (L4, L5, L10). The L55 twist is L12 again. Introduce one more distinct "insight verb" per chapter, such as cell placement, order sequencing, or spending on a generator versus a crate.
2. Late introductions. Jars (L40), Sweep (L47), wrapped trunks (L53), recipe stacking (L57) and chain orders (L58) all land in the last 40%. Chain orders and mixed recipes are then required at full strength in the L62-L64 finales. Move recipe stacking to about L20-24. Move a simple chain-order teach to about L45. Move L48 (an easy Sweep repeat) to after the L52 boss, or replace it.
3. Difficulty and rhythm. Casual win rates mostly follow the sawtooth, with a dip, then a teach, then a breather. The last third averages about 65.3%, marginally over the cap, because L44, L47, L48 and L60 sit at 81-85%. Tighten those slightly. There are no easy-level streaks of 3 or more. Hidden early fatal moves hurt in L9 (trap 0.5) and L10 (trap 0.25), exactly where players are still learning. Story beats (L5, 10, 20, 25, 30, 35) vanish after L35 despite the "every ~5 levels" hook, so add beats around L40, 45, 50, 55 and 60.
4. Session length and board usage. Levels are 5-14 moves on small boards, far shorter than the stated 3-10 minute session. Use the 7x9 grid for at least the boss levels (the L64 diya shape does this well), and let mid-game levels have 15-25 moves.
